# Prologue

# Chapter 1 – The Slingshot with Caw

As Ersatzica held the slingshot, a thought crossed her mind. It was a regular wooden slingshot with a leather band, originally treated with proper varnish to make it last, but has since seen some better days. For one, the leather band was snapped in too.

“Pointy rock,” Wryonin, her brother, had said, shrugging and entrusting the slingshot into Ersatzica’s care.

Repairing the band was near impossible, but replacing the band—that could be done. But the thought of doing so gave Ersatzica inspiration to do something extra.

Ersatzica, a teenage wolf girl, stood in the middle of her workshop, where she brings her ideas to life and brings out the best version of objects that others bring her.

This slingshot in particular was very special to her, because it was special to someone she loved: her brother.

On a giant sheet of paper, she unwound the knots of the band as she detached it from the slingshot, meticulously drawing the curves as she did so. She laid the untied band flat on the paper.

Then she reached up into the cupboards and pulled out a leather sheet. She flipped her trusty screwdriver to the leather knife side and cut a strip of leather off the sheet, matching the snapped band as closely as possible.

Lastly, she tied the band onto the slingshot, matching the knots that were previously there.

Admiring her handy work, a thought crossed her mind.

See, Wryonin uses his slingshot to hunt, and he’s quite good at it. And when he goes hunting, he hunts with his raven. Or well, he used to. It’s been awhile since his raven died. Wryonin hasn’t really been the same since.

And it’s really faint, but if you look closely at the slingshot, at the right angle, you can see a flash of blue light glimmer off the slingshot, as if the sun is reflecting off it at the perfect angle. But you can see this even in the dark.

It has a certain energy to it, that’s hard to describe. But it *feels* like the same energy as his raven had. Like his raven is in there, calling to him, saying it’s going to be ok.

So, the thought. There’s no decoration on the slingshot. There’s no indication on the outside that his raven is still with him through this item. And I don’t think he’s yet aware that Alpha has given him this gift of grief. So it might make him happy to see that He has.

Ersatzica took out a can of black paint and a brush. She etched a quick sketch of a raven’s silhouette. Then she inked the sketch with the brush. She set the slingshot on the table to dry.

The faint blue shimmer still shone behind the paint, this time a little brighter despite being partially covered.

“You’re welcome.” Ersatzica beamed as she stroked the side of the slingshot, in much the same way that Wryonin used to stroke his raven’s head. She nuzzled the slingshot with her wolf nose, careful to not touch the wet paint.

Then she set the slingshot on the “display” shelf next to the other completed items waiting to be picked up. Among them, a metal war hammer leaned in the corner.

A wolf howl rang through the town. It was the hourly howl. Ersatzica looked out the window, to find that it was mid-afternoon.

Her neck hair stood on end. “It’s almost time to go meet Uncle Yotleph! And I haven’t fixed his satchel yet!”

Uncle Yotleph, Awoomoon’s dad’s brother, never came to town. He always met Ersatzica and her siblings by the river, but only once a month. These monthly meetings were special to her and her siblings, as he would give them each a gift and a task to complete before the next meeting.

And last month’s task, for Ersatzica, was to repair this satchel. She thought the repair would be easy, so she put it off and put it off. Now it’s the day of, and she can’t put it off anymore.

She assessed the state of the satchel. There was a huge tear on the bottom of the bag, rendering it unusable. She could stitch it with thread, but sewing into leather was time consuming. She knew that was the right way to do it, but she didn’t have time.

So instead, she opened a bottle of raccoon spit that Yotleph had previously given her, and applied it to one end of the tear. She pressed the two sides together, and blew on the seam until the raccoon spit sealed it shut. She tried pulling it apart afterwards, but it didn’t re-tear. Check. Satchel fixed. Check.

She put the lid back on the vial of raccoon spit, and lamented that she only had 3 bottles left. It was a rare and valuable resource, but it was worth using to not disappoint Yotleph.

There was a knock at the door.

Ersatzica’s ears pinned back. “Come in.”

Wryonin opened the door and entered the room, muzzle pointed down. “Hi, Ersatzica.”

“Hi, Wryonin. Catch anything today?”

“No, the traps aren’t working.”

“Well you are a hunter, not a trapper.”

“I’m not sure if I am anymore.”

“You are, Wryonin. You still are.”

“Hmmm.” Wryonin’s looked up at Ersatzica. “Is it done?”

Ersatzica pulled the slingshot off the wall, carefully holding it so Wryonin couldn’t see the paint. “Let me see.” She blew on the paint and touched her clawed finger to it. The paint didn’t come off or smear. Check. “Yep!” She held it out to Wryonin.

“Thanks, sis.” Wryonin took it. He examined it, instantly finding the painted raven. “Ersatzica! What is this!”

“It’s a painting of Caw.”

“What!”

“Your raven.”

“I know my raven’s name. Why is there a painting on my slingshot?”

“You’ve been really down lately since your Raven died, but you don’t seem to realize that Alpha has given you a grieving gift.”

“What grieving gift?”

“This slingshot!” Ersatzica held it up. “Caw lives on in it, and I thought it’d make you happy to have a visual reminder of him with this painting.”

“I didn’t ask for the painting!” Wryonin barked, teeth bared. “I asked for the strap to be fixed! Now the pristine condition that grandpa left it in is tampered with! It’ll take me hours to wash it off.” He scratched at the painting with his claws, and with his teeth, but it didn’t appear to be even chipped. “Thanks a lot.”

“I thought you’d find it comforting—”

“Well, you thought wrong.” Wryonin grabbed the slingshot in his mouth and ran off on all fours. He didn’t even bother to close the door on his way out.

Ersatzica grumbled over her workspace. She went to pick up her screwdriver, but she couldn’t.

Why does he have to be so ungrateful? She was only trying to help. And how does Caw feel, knowing that Wryonin doesn’t accept his grieving gift? That must feel awful. Ersatzica’s heart sank for Caw. He lived his entire life devoted to Wryonin, died for Wryonin, then essentially got rejected by Wryonin. That’s awful.

The next hour howl came and went, but Ersatzica was still stuck in her workshop unable to do anything.

That is, until a certain energy approached the workshop from outside. It bounced and bopped its way through the town, causing cheers and commotion from the wolves outside all along the way. Howls rang out as it came.

Ersatzica turned to the door. It couldn’t stop what was behind it. With one big burst, the door flung open and bounced so hard against the wall that it came back to fully close. But in that brief moment it was open, the energy came inside.

It was Mudrich! The all-white-but-always-mud-covered wolf runt lept from floor tile to floor tile, dropping flakes of dirt each time she landed. “Ersie!” She ran right up to her and tackled her, licking her face. “It’s today! It’s today!” Her tail wagged uncontrollably, knocking jars of tools and leather scraps off the nearby shelves.

“I love you, Muddy.” Ersie put up a hand to shield her face from Muddy’s tongue. “But what did I say about personal space?”

“Oh, right. That’s a thing.” Muddy stood on two legs, but her wagging tail still bounced her body around. “Yotleph is coming!”

“I know! I’m so excited!”

“What do you think he’ll bring us this time?”

“I’m hoping for more racoon spit, I’m almost out.”

“I hope I get another skull.” Mudrich salivated. “Or at least a tooth, maybe a fang.”

“Maybe. Did you finish your task?”

Mudrich barked. “I found ten round stones!”

“Where are they?”

“In a hole.” Mudrich shrugged. “Speaking of, did you finish it?”

“Well, kind of.” Ersatzica picked up the satchel. “I didn’t have time to stitch—”

“Not that, my hammer!”

“Oh that. It’s right over there.”

Mudrich lept to the display corner. “It’s so shiny!” Mudrich grabbed—

“Wait!” Ersatzica stopped her. “Before you grab it, what are you *not* going to do?”

“Uh…” Mudrich whimpered. “Oh! Swing it around the shop?”

“Right. Swing around it around the shop. Wait no, I mean, *don’t*—as in do *not* swing it around the shop.”

“Right.” Mudrich grabbed the hammer with two hands, picking it up and swinging it over her shoulder. For such a short wolf, she was very stocky and muscular. She can even beat Wryonin at arm wrestling. “This feels so good!”

“I’m glad you like it!” Ersatzica beamed. “I cleaned off the rust, added some protective sealant, and—”

Mudrich swung the hammer in the middle of the room, hitting the display shelf with her tail.

“Mudrich!”

Mudrich rested the hammer on the ground. “Sorry.”

“And I made some wooden stakes from some scraps.”

“What are these for?”

“You really need to not leave your hammer outdoors overnight, especially when it rains. You see, the rain is a catalyst in the oxidizing process, which creates rust, and—”

Mudrich lifted the hammer off the ground.

“Muddy!”

“I’m sorry. Sorry.”

“And when that happens, rust forms—”

Mudrich yawned. She leaned her muzzle on her hammer, eyelids heavy.

Ersatzica rolled her eyes. “Just keep it indoors from now on, ok?”

“But you said not to indoors.”

“I said not to swing it indoors. You can store it indoors.”

“But then how will I know where I left off with my tunnels?”

Ersatzica held up the wooden stakes.

“I can’t dig with those.”

“No, but you can use them to mark where your tunnels are.”

“Oh….” The gears in Muddy’s head were turning. “Oh!” She grabbed the stakes. “These are amazing, Ersie! Thank you so much!” She grabbed the wooden stakes in her mouth, salivating.

“I’m glad you like them.” Ersie smiled. She gave her shorter sister a hug.

Mudrich then mumbled something unintelligible.

“I can’t understand you when you have stuff in your mouth.” Ersie said. “But I think you’re talking about going to the river to meet Uncle Yotleph?”

Mudrich nodded her head.

“Let’s go!” Ersie grabbed the satchel, loaded it with her screwdriver and a pillow from the display case, and followed Muddy out the door.
